# René — The Researched Raw Feeder (RRF)

> "I've already fixed my dog with food. I don't need another product, I need one that earns its place in the bowl."

*The hardest avatar in the database to convert — see strategist note.*

## One-line summary
Discerning, evangelical raw feeder who has already solved her dog's health issues with diet; not desperate, allergic to marketing, and competing against her own DIY supplement shelf.

## Who they are
René didn't drift into raw feeding — she researched her way in, usually after a sick dog that kibble and the vet couldn't fix (chronic itch, recurring ear infections, hot spots, loose stools, dull coat). Someone in a Facebook group suggested raw, she saw improvement within weeks, and was converted for life. She reads every ingredient label, knows the difference between "pet food" and "pet feed" (Act 36 of 1947), and treats the 2025–2026 RCL Foods recalls as proof she was right. She views kibble as junk food and "Big Pet Food" — and the vets she believes it sponsors — with deep suspicion. Crucially, she has often already solved her dog's health problem with food. She is not desperate. She is discerning, a little evangelical, and braced for a fight the moment anything smells like marketing.

## Core problem
Not a crisis — a trust and optimisation problem. She believes she's already fixed the big stuff with diet, so any new product has to earn its place in the bowl. Her real friction is credibility: she assumes a vet-branded supplement is "Big Pet Food" in disguise, and she's allergic to being sold to or lectured. At the same time she quietly knows her DIY bowl has gaps — EPA/DHA, joint protection, micronutrient balance — and she's open to completing it, as long as the product is human-grade, transparent, and never implies her raw diet was incomplete.

## Core pains & frustrations
- **Vet distrust** — only ~36% of raw feeders rate their vet's nutritional advice as highly trustworthy (vs ~64% of kibble feeders). A vet brand is a barrier, not a badge, until reframed.
- **"Kibble is junk food" worldview** — anything that looks/sounds like commercial pet food is rejected on sight.
- **Ingredient scrutiny** — reads every panel. Human-grade, wild-caught, no fillers/additives, or she walks.
- **Already supplementing** — salmon oil, kelp, green-lipped mussel, kefir, golden paste. You compete with her DIY shelf, not kibble.
- **Hype fatigue / been burned** — anecdote-driven, distrustful of industry-funded science. Promises without mechanism read as marketing.
- **Identity threat** — will reject anything implying raw is "not enough." Frame as optimising, never replacing.

## What she wants to hear
- "Even raw-fed dogs are usually short on EPA/DHA unless you're feeding oily fish several times a week — here's the actual concentration."
- "Wild-caught, human-grade, 35% omega-3, no fillers, nothing you can't pronounce."
- "A topper that completes the bowl, not a replacement for it."
- "I add this to my own dogs' raw and here's exactly why."

## Awareness level
Solution Aware / Product Aware — highly informed, values-driven.

## Products that fit
- **Omega-3 — PRIMARY.** Position as the high-concentration, wild-caught, no-filler topper that out-performs the supermarket salmon oil she already uses (35% concentration, EPA/DHA, corrects the omega-6:omega-3 ratio).
- **Joint Support — SECONDARY.** Cross-sell through the green-lipped mussel angle (already her language; big/young breeds are joint-risk).
- **Do NOT lead with Dental Powder** — she believes raw meaty bones handle teeth.

## Strategist notes
Won on ingredient transparency + mechanism + values alignment; lost the instant she smells "Big Pet Food." Lead with ingredient honesty and human-grade sourcing — not vet authority. When using Dr. Amir, frame him as the raw-friendly, real-food vet who feeds this to his own dogs, never as the establishment correcting her. Above all: never imply her raw diet is incomplete — the only safe frame is completing / optimising the bowl.

## Language guardrails
**Use:** wild-caught • human-grade • EPA/DHA concentration • no fillers / no additives • species-appropriate • topper • completes the bowl • ingredient panel • mechanism
**Avoid:** vet-recommended (as the lead) • complete & balanced (kibble's slogan) • prescription • "replaces" • clinical-only framing • anything that talks down to her or defends the vet/pet-food industry
