# Guilty Grace — The Defeated Daily Carer (GG)

> "I know I should brush. I just can't."

## One-line summary
Well-meaning owner who has failed at brushing for years and feels chronic guilt; the zero-friction "sprinkle on food" promise is a direct match for her dominant unmet desire.

## Who they are
Grace was told at her puppy's first vet visit to brush every day. She tried. The dog resisted. She got a few sessions in, then life got in the way, then the dog got worse about tolerating it, and she gradually stopped. Every annual check-up the vet mentions dental hygiene. She nods, feels the familiar wave of guilt. She has tried finger brushes, different toothpastes, different timings, dental chews (swallowed in seconds), water additives (made her panic about dehydration). Nothing has worked. She is not a neglectful owner — she has been failed by the tools.

## Core problem
Her dog's breath is bad. The dog licks her face and she turns away and feels terrible. The vet keeps recommending a R5,000+ anaesthetic dental clean, which she's been putting off. She carries daily low-grade guilt about not brushing. The product's zero-friction promise — sprinkle on food, done — is a direct match for her dominant unmet desire: to feel like a good pet parent without the daily battle.

## Core pains & frustrations
- **Chronic guilt** — has been failing at the brushing routine for years.
- **Close-contact embarrassment** — dog sleeps in the bed and the breath is waking her up.
- **Defeat** — "I've tried everything and nothing works."
- **Vet judgement** — feels judged at every check-up, even if the vet is kind about it.
- **Anaesthetic cost aversion** — R3,000–R8,000+ is a lot; putting it off is quietly becoming a bigger problem.
- **Zero-friction need** — her dog will not tolerate anything requiring her hands near his mouth.

## What she wants to hear
- "You literally just sprinkle it on the food."
- "He doesn't even know it's happening."
- "I feel like such a bad pet parent."
- "My dog's breath could clear a room." / "Death breath."
- Permission-granting copy. Dark humour welcomed. Absolution, not instruction.

## Awareness level
Problem Aware — needs relief and permission, not education.

## Products that fit
- **Dental Powder — PRIMARY.** Grace is a primary dental avatar.

## Strategist notes
Lead with permission ("brushing is impossible, we get it") and zero-friction ("sprinkle, done"). Close-contact framing (dog in the bed, morning breath) outperforms "dental health" framing. Avoid clinical language — she wants relief, not a lecture.
